# Brolly Exam Prep Competitor and Organic Growth Strategy

## Executive conclusion

Brolly should not try to beat Testbook, Adda247, Oliveboard or Physics Wallah by imitating their breadth. Those companies compete with enormous inventories, multilingual apps, live classes, paid bundles, learner counts and years of accumulated brand demand. A new domain cannot close that gap by publishing hundreds of generic “exam syllabus” pages or by claiming to be India’s largest platform.

The viable route is narrower and more defensible: become the most trustworthy free diagnostic-and-correction platform for a selected set of examinations. Each target exam should connect an official-source guide, a realistic fixed mock, transparent scoring, section and topic diagnosis, a correction plan, focused lessons, and a fresh retest. The product should demonstrate this loop on the page rather than merely describe it.

The current site has a strong technical base: prerendered HTML, descriptive routes, canonical URLs, breadcrumbs, structured data, crawlable navigation and substantial topic lessons. Its largest weaknesses are product depth, proof, freshness operations, real identity signals, Hindi coverage, distribution and authority. The existing search footprint also shows URL-quality problems: Google has surfaced generic fallback pages for routes that do not contain the promised exam-specific material. Those weaknesses matter more than adding another layer of generic copy.

No ethical strategy can guarantee position one. Google states that even compliant pages are not guaranteed to be crawled, indexed or served, and its systems prioritize helpful, reliable, people-first content rather than content produced mainly to manipulate rankings ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). The objective should therefore be measurable share growth across chosen query clusters, with #1 rankings emerging page by page.

## Market and competitor set

### Tier 1: scaled incumbents

**Testbook** is the scale benchmark. Its public homepage presents large test inventories, free-test counts, multilingual availability, user counts, detailed analysis, live tests and named selection outcomes. Search-visible examples include hundreds or thousands of tests per exam family and support for English, Hindi and several regional languages. Its advantage is not prose; it is the combined proof of inventory, participation, result history and product depth ([Testbook](https://testbook.com/?hl=en_IN)).

**Adda247** competes as a bundle ecosystem. Public product pages combine live and recorded classes, mock tests, ebooks, printed books, daily practice, doubt support and exam-target batches. This lets Adda247 capture informational, transactional and branded demand at many stages of preparation. Its public pages also expose exact bundle quantities and pricing, which creates tangible product evidence even where page quality is uneven ([Adda247 teaching pack](https://shop.adda247.com/products/teaching-exams-ka-maha-pack), [Adda247 CUET course](https://www.adda247.com/product-onlineliveclasses/96203/cuet-science-complete-online-coaching)).

**Oliveboard** is the strongest direct benchmark for analytical mock testing. Its test-series page emphasizes current patterns, full-length, sectional, topic and advanced mocks, English/Hindi availability, instant analytics, time spent, weak areas and comparison. It also displays named rank outcomes and a broad list of central and state exam categories ([Oliveboard test series](https://www.oliveboard.in/test-series/)).

**Physics Wallah** uses its education brand and commerce ecosystem to cross-sell courses, books and test passes. Public pages enumerate mock types, exam coverage, previous-year papers, chapter tests, reattempts and detailed solutions. Its store also turns books and PYQ compilations into search landing pages with concrete quantities and current-year framing ([PW government test pass](https://www.pw.live/ssc/test-series), [PW government exam books](https://store.pw.live/govt-entrance-exams?checkout-language=english-checkout)).

### Tier 2: focused product competitors

**AglaSem Mock Test** offers a simple free-test proposition with answer keys, solutions and performance reports. Its focused banking page shows that a smaller inventory can still create a coherent landing page when every listed test is immediately usable ([AglaSem banking mocks](https://mocktest.aglasem.com/group/banking)).

**Pareeksha** differentiates around real-exam simulation, negative marking, Hindi/English support, percentile, rooms and a low-cost pass. Its public copy makes the product concrete through fixed papers, interface details, analytics and visible inventory claims ([Pareeksha](https://pareeksha.in/)).

**MockCampus** narrows further to banking and leads with a clear free/no-paywall promise. It demonstrates the testing interface and reports accuracy, skipped questions and negative-marking impact. This is a useful example of positioning a smaller product against broad incumbents ([MockCampus](https://mockcampus.com/)).

**FreeMockTest**, **Repeater Mock**, **Kranti** and similar emerging sites compete for free-test and long-tail exam searches. Their authority may be lower than the incumbents, but their narrower claims, simpler task flows and rapidly expanding inventories make them relevant day-to-day SERP competitors ([FreeMockTest](https://freemocktest.co/), [Repeater Mock](https://www.repeatermock.com/), [Kranti](https://www.kraanti.in/)).

## Brolly strengths

### 1. A coherent information architecture

The site organizes exams into syllabus, pattern, previous papers, mocks, preparation and study material. Topic lessons are reused across relevant exams instead of being copied into many near-identical pages. This is conceptually stronger than the loosely connected article inventories common in the sector.

Google says a logical structure and relevant internal links help users and search engines understand page relationships; it also notes that links are a major discovery mechanism ([SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide?rd=2), [sitelinks guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/sitelinks?hl=en)). Brolly already has the foundation for strong exam-topic relationships.

### 2. Substantial concept content

Many study topics go beyond thin definitions and include methods, examples, tables, practice questions and FAQs. This is the strongest existing organic asset. The next improvement is to attach visible reviewers, primary references where facts change, and original diagrams or demonstrations.

### 3. Server-visible output

The production build prerenders every route, gives canonical pages unique titles and descriptions, and emits structured data. Search engines do not need to wait for client-side rendering to see the primary content. This removes a common technical failure but is only an eligibility foundation, not a ranking moat.

### 4. A potentially differentiating learning loop

Brolly’s best message is: diagnose, identify the cause, study the exact weakness, and retest. Competitors often provide analytics, but their scale can make the next action feel like another catalog choice. Brolly can own a more opinionated correction workflow if the product truly implements it.

## Critical gaps

### 1. Product proof is far behind the copy

Eight shortened mock tests cannot support a broad “complete preparation” position against platforms showing hundreds of tests per exam. The site must not attempt to solve this with claims. It needs more usable papers, sections and questions.

For the first three exam clusters, the minimum credible inventory is:

- five fixed full-pattern or proportionally shortened diagnostic mocks;
- ten sectional tests per major scored section;
- one worked recent-paper analysis per available official paper;
- topic tags on every question;
- explanation quality checks and a published correction policy;
- saved attempts, time per question, error categories and a generated correction queue.

### 2. No strong language strategy

Testbook, Oliveboard and Adda247 prominently expose Hindi and other languages. Brolly currently uses English while making pan-India claims. Machine-translating 455 pages would create a maintenance and trust problem. Begin with Hindi UI, instructions, explanations and landing pages for one cluster, reviewed by a fluent subject editor. Use distinct `/hi/` URLs, correct `hreflang`, self-canonicals and synchronized change tracking.

### 3. Weak demonstrable expertise

The site lists subject areas and generic “experienced faculty” labels without names, credentials, review responsibility or revision history. Google’s people-first guidance explicitly asks whether content makes readers want to trust it through sourcing, author background and demonstrable expertise ([Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

Do not invent biographies. Collect real names, headshots, qualifications, exams taught, years of relevant teaching, and review domains. Add a public editorial policy explaining how official notifications, answer keys and corrections are handled. Each exam guide should show “Reviewed by,” last substantive review date and official sources.

### 4. Freshness claims need operations

Phrases such as “current pattern,” “updated daily” and “latest” create an obligation. Brolly needs an owner and verification record for every time-sensitive page. A date should change only when the substance changes; Google warns against changing dates merely to appear fresh ([Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

Create a source ledger with page URL, claim type, official source URL, checked date, next review trigger and reviewer. When a commission publishes a notification or corrigendum, update affected pages and show a short change note.

### 5. Authority and distribution are undeveloped

On-page completeness cannot replace third-party recognition. Brolly needs assets that teachers, colleges, libraries, journalists and aspirant communities would genuinely reference:

- an exam-pattern change tracker sourced to official notices;
- downloadable topic-frequency datasets derived from official papers;
- a negative-marking decision calculator;
- a free score-diagnostic report users can share;
- state recruitment-source directories;
- accessible charts showing question distribution across years;
- correction logs for disputed questions and official final keys.

Outreach should target institutions that benefit from the asset, not purchased links or mass guest posts. Inauthentic mentions are specifically rejected as a durable generative-search tactic in Google’s AI guidance ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

### 6. Performance and front-end scale

The current production build emits a JavaScript bundle of roughly 4 MB uncompressed and about 1.17 MB gzip. Primary content is prerendered, which protects initial crawlability, but mobile interaction and Core Web Vitals can still suffer. Route-level data/code separation should be planned carefully so prerendering remains intact. Measure field data before and after rather than optimizing to a synthetic score alone.

### 7. Search result quality needs monitoring

Live search results have exposed generic Brolly fallback pages for state-exam-style URLs. A fallback that resembles the homepage can be interpreted as a soft 404 and spends crawl attention on a page without a clear promise. Every sitemap URL should map to a real content record; unsupported routes should return a genuine not-found response or remain out of the index until built.

## Positioning to beat rather than copy competitors

### Recommended category

**The free exam diagnostic that tells you exactly what to fix next.**

This position is narrower than “all exam preparation” and more valuable than “free mock tests.” It connects the product and the content library:

1. Choose an exam and stage.
2. Take a fixed diagnostic under realistic conditions.
3. Receive accuracy, pacing, section, topic and error-cause analysis.
4. Get a seven-day correction queue linked to Brolly lessons and drills.
5. Retest on a fresh equivalent set.
6. See whether the diagnosed weakness actually improved.

The defensible asset is not the article describing this loop. It is the longitudinal dataset and product experience created by repeated attempts.

### Initial beachhead

Prioritize three clusters rather than the entire catalog:

1. **SSC CGL and adjacent SSC exams**: high shared subject overlap and strong demand, but extremely competitive. Use CGL as the flagship and reuse topic infrastructure for CHSL, MTS and CPO only where patterns genuinely overlap.
2. **Banking prelims**: SBI PO and IBPS PO are already live, and sectional timing makes diagnosis particularly valuable. Add SBI Clerk/IBPS Clerk only after the PO loop is complete.
3. **Telangana and Bihar state recruitment**: regional specificity offers more whitespace than national head terms. Official-source quality and bilingual/local context can differentiate Brolly from national catalogs.

Do not lead with UPSC until Brolly can support current affairs sourcing, mains answer evaluation and demonstrable subject expertise at the level the audience expects.

## Search architecture

Each priority exam should have one canonical pillar and a limited set of pages with distinct jobs:

- exam overview: eligibility, stages, official sources and route map;
- syllabus: exact scope plus paper-derived weighting;
- pattern: current rules and stage/merit consequences;
- previous papers: official files, final keys and original trend analysis;
- mock tests: live inventory, methodology and sample report;
- preparation: diagnostic-based sequence for different baselines;
- study material: only the lessons relevant to this exam, with reasons;
- notification/update pages only when a real event exists.

Avoid generating pages for every keyword permutation. Google says its systems understand synonyms and that scaled pages made to manipulate Search can violate spam policy ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

## English and Hindi plan

Hindi should be a product and editorial capability, not a translation switch added for keyword coverage.

Phase one:

- Hindi test instructions and answer explanations for SSC CGL and one state cluster;
- Hindi landing pages written/reviewed by a fluent editor;
- bilingual terminology glossary for exam-specific words;
- language choice persisted across tests;
- translation QA for numbers, negation, options and legal/official wording.

Phase two:

- bilingual topic lessons chosen from actual Hindi query and test-error data;
- Hindi video or diagram explanations for topics with strong visual value;
- regional-language pilots only after a named reviewer and maintenance workflow exist.

## 90-day execution plan

### Days 1–14: measurement and trust

- Connect/verify the canonical domain in Search Console.
- Export 16 months and the last 3 months by query, page, country and device.
- Segment branded vs non-branded and group queries by exam and intent.
- Remove or noindex unsupported fallback URLs.
- Replace placeholders and unverifiable claims; the fake footer phone has already been removed locally.
- Publish real editorial, correction and sourcing policies.
- Add genuine reviewer profiles only after collecting evidence.
- Establish baseline field performance and conversion events.

### Days 15–45: three complete exam loops

- Choose one flagship exam from SSC, banking and state recruitment.
- Build the minimum test inventory and topic tagging described above.
- Add saved attempts, time-per-question data and correction queues.
- Publish one original recent-paper study per flagship exam.
- Add official source ledgers and visible review dates.
- Launch reviewed Hindi support for the highest-volume flagship.

### Days 46–90: distribution and iteration

- Publish the pattern-change tracker and topic-frequency datasets.
- Conduct targeted outreach to teachers, colleges and aspirant communities.
- Improve pages already receiving impressions in positions 4–20 before creating more pages.
- Compare Search Console periods by query cluster, page, device and country.
- Rewrite titles only where impressions are meaningful and CTR is weak.
- Consolidate pages that compete for the same query and strengthen internal links to the selected canonical.
- Run usability interviews with at least ten active aspirants per flagship cluster.

## Measurement framework

Google’s Search Console Performance report supports analysis by query, page, country, device and date, and can identify high-impression/low-CTR opportunities ([Performance report overview](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en), [common use cases](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17010961)). Position should be treated as a trend, not a single universal rank, because results vary by location, device, time and history.

Track weekly:

- non-branded impressions and clicks by priority cluster;
- count of priority queries in positions 1–3, 4–10 and 11–20;
- CTR by query/page pair;
- indexed canonical pages and soft-404/excluded patterns;
- diagnostic starts and completions;
- solution-review rate;
- correction-plan engagement;
- seven- and thirty-day return rate;
- retest improvement by topic and error class;
- referring domains earned by original assets;
- mobile Core Web Vitals field pass rates.

The first success target is not “rank #1 for competitive exam preparation.” It is: win several narrow, high-intent pages; turn those visitors into completed diagnostics; prove improvement; earn references; and expand from demonstrated authority.

- Obtain Search Console exports and build a query-to-page opportunity table.
- Remove unsupported pages from the sitemap/index or supply real records.
- Replace generic faculty cards with real identities or clearly label them as subject desks.
- Add editorial methodology, correction policy and official-source review fields.
- Remove all remaining unverifiable claims of “full-length,” “latest,” “daily” or “detailed analysis” where the live product does not support them.

### P1

- Complete the SSC CGL diagnostic loop and five-test inventory.
- Complete the IBPS/SBI prelims diagnostic loop with sectional timing.
- Create one regional state cluster with verified official sources and reviewed Hindi content.
- Add attempt persistence and meaningful topic/time analytics.
- Create pattern-change and PYQ-frequency linkable assets.

### P2

- Split route/data bundles while preserving prerendered content.
- Add image and video assets where they genuinely explain a method.
- Expand only from Search Console demand and product usage evidence.
- Build partnerships with educators and institutions around free diagnostics.
